# Direction Notes: Chapter 7

## What is not working well enough

- This chapter knows exactly how the operation works, but it does not always know where the emotional emphasis belongs.
- Too much of the middle reads like procedure when it should read like pressure.
- José has real stakes here, but they are spread across too many fronts without enough shaping, so the chapter can feel flatter than the events actually are.
- The chapter keeps delivering important developments, but not all of them land with enough difference in weight.

## What I want the chapter to do better

- Make José feel more present as a man under increasing strain, not just as a competent operator.
- Let success feel costly. Every win in this chapter should tighten the screws somewhere else.
- Make the chapter feel like escalation, not accumulation.
- Keep the industrial and technical realism, but only in the places where it creates suspense, character, or consequence.

## José

- José should feel practical, capable, tired, proud, worried, and a little trapped by his own success.
- His motivation is not just "save the business." It is family, pride, obligation, momentum, and the fear of slipping backward now that he has finally built something real.
- The difference between how strong he is at work and how absent he has become at home should come through more clearly.

## Pacing

- Compress or simplify technical explanation when it is not actively making the scene more tense.
- Keep the salvage material vivid, but make sure it plays as drama, not as a manual.
- Let the biggest beats breathe:
  - the foreclosure pressure
  - the first major payout
  - the failed recovery
  - the rescue attempt
  - Tobias Raines at the end

## What must stay

- Do not radically change the plot.
- Keep the overall event chain:
  - successful recoveries
  - financial pressure at home
  - titanium sale and new opportunity
  - shackle failure
  - return rescue mission
  - hardware discussion
  - Felipe and the sheet-metal expansion problem
  - cash relief at home
  - Tobias Raines arriving as the closing move
- Keep continuity with chapters 1-6 and chapter 8.

## What I especially want to preserve

- The opening image of the fireballs and the recovery setup
- The sense that José has built something bold and improvised and real
- The lost-bowl recovery as a genuine stress test
- The feeling that the business is suddenly becoming bigger than José can comfortably contain
- Tobias Raines as the point where the chapter opens into a larger world

## Rewrite priorities

1. Give the chapter a cleaner dramatic spine.
2. Make José's internal pressure more legible at the major turns.
3. Cut procedural density where it is slowing the chapter down.
4. Make the second half feel more cumulative and less episodic.
5. Make the Tobias ending land hard.
