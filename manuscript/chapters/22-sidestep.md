“I need to reserve the Oaks Boardroom again this evening. I will be having a guest from out of town. And he will need a room.”

“Wonderful, Dr. Sarai. We are happy to set that up. We trust your Cyprus Suite is still meeting your needs. I will set up his room next to yours.”

“Thank you.”

Raines arrived late. Elin met him at the airport. “Tobias, always a pleasure. I have your room key.”

“If I go to my room, I will sleep for a week. Show me the boardroom.” 

After the taxi ride, she led him straight there. Raines keyed up a slide on his slate, projecting it on the wall. “Have you seen this\!” Raines exclaimed, pounding his fist against the wall. “JRC is building palaces, not launch facilities. I read their entire quarterly report. Lovely pictures of their headquarters building and the streets of Baracoa. Shots of the JRI campus. Jovian Research Institute. A college? Who even authorized that morass? No mention of Punta de Maisi or launch facilities. The entire report reads like a travelogue. They have had a year, and they’ve done nothing.”

“They are diplomats. This is what they do.”

Raines didn’t stop. “We need action. Villas don’t power fusion. Right now, the one thing that is keeping us operational is rapid growth on the Moon, but it is not making enough to feed even one terawatt tokaplex. I have prepaid orders for ten.”

“The Moon is working hard. Tan called me recently with new designs for lunar sweepers. She thinks some sites can be twice as productive with them. I introduced her to Case.”

“Well done, Elin. Tan is an absolute engine. But even a factor of two won’t get us where we need to be. Do you know if Tan has a Kagutsuchi Vortex yet?” 

“Yes, two were delivered to the Moon. Derrin powered one up. Mateo mentioned he is commissioning another fast boat to keep up with the increased titanium production. When I told him Mars and Ceres would be getting more power, too, he said he was going to order three. Manufacturing may not be the real constraint with them, though. With the fissile material ban, General Atomics told me they are retiring their one remaining fission-powered freighter after this next run.”

“So we are back to individually commissioned hydrolox ferries? Those are so slow.”

“It’s worse than that. Tan’s titanium production is tied to her *³He* production. Andrei will have a much harder time scaling than Mateo. His current fleet is built on repurposed used ships. I don’t know if he can handle the increased volume.” 

Raines looked back at the projection on the wall of the JRC quarterly report, his eyes flashing in anger. “This cannot work. We need interplanetary transit. JRC is not going to step up. What are we going to do?”

“We are likely going to deliver another Kagutsuchi Vortex every two months. You might talk to Andrei. If he can afford to commission new fusion-powered ships, he could improve lunar service, and maybe open up mid-system service.”

“I don’t know what the lead time will be, but that gives me hope.”

Raines brought up a new chart. “Elin, I don’t know where you are with the terawatt Tokaplexes, but I have a lynch mob of impatient customers who want that power now. I have some *³He* in storage. Are you ready to start on China?”

“I only have one construction area for the terawatt tokaplexes, but I keep it busy. You want me to ship it now?”

Raines stepped away from the projection and looked out over the bay. He could see Elin’s artificial atoll in the distance, where the terawatt tokaplex was being built. “I believe Tan will deliver five tons per month soon. When she does, I want you to be ready to ship. And when lunar production hits ten tons per month, ship another.”

“If you are willing to dip into your reserves, I can deliver that, but these will drain your reserves fast.”

Still facing out the window, he began talking softly. “Elin, let me be frank. This business, with machines you built, is immensely profitable. Even so, we cannot afford to go to Jupiter. JRC has the money and the charter to do it, but it doesn’t seem primed to act. They have five projects in their charter. They need to make heavy lift cheaper, so they can get their equipment into space. They need to work out interplanetary transport so they can get their equipment to Jupiter. They need to work out *³He* collection, and they need to figure out how to get that *³He* out of the Jovian gravity well. And they need to paint murals on their buildings. They are making excellent progress on that last project.”

He turned back to face her. “What I want us to do is to help them. If they have an idea, I want you to look it over and offer some suggestions. If they want to build something, we find a company to build it and, behind the scenes, cover part of the cost to make it cheaper for JRC. I want them to look like the luckiest team alive, because we will be backing them. We won’t get any credit. All our help will be disguised as third-party stuff. But we can sidestep their bureaucratic limitations, and, maybe, actually get somewhere.”

Elin was quiet for a moment. “Tobias, you know I will do whatever I can. But, irrespective of what I do, they may not succeed for decades.”

“I know, but I refuse to let that thought limit me. We need to make this work.”

“Right now, I am getting the tokaplexes built. The demand for the ten gigawatt units remains very high, and the terawatt units still require refinement. I am also helping Kagutsuchi build their vortexes. And, most importantly, I help Gonçalo Silva keep the operational plants working. Time spent on JRC projects will necessarily slow other work.”

“I know, but I also know none of these systems will work without Jovian helium.”

Room service delivered some Gulf Shrimp Cocktails. They watched the ships move across the bay as they ate.
