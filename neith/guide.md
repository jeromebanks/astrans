    Naelis was piloting a new freighter, high above the forests of Meriddon. The wings extended thirty feet from teh hull.  A marvel of modern engineering.  He remembered the planes he had made as a kid, with foot long wings, crashing int “o his friends, trying to knock each other out of the sky. He got knocked down more than most. Who would have guessed he would become a test pilot.
    As he flew higher, the fog obscured the forest. He had flown up here before. The world was empty, except for the great light. But this freighter was designed to go higher.  He pulled the stick back and hoped for the best.  No one had been this high before.  They didn’t know if the engine would even work at these pressures.  That’s why the world needed test pilots.
    As he ascended, he could see the lights of Tavilly, two hundred miles away. He;d been up a long time, and the second light was showing.  As it came into focus, he was amazed to see smaller lights, too, tiny spots in the sky. He took out his phone and took several pictures before flying back down.

   Avel looked at his report. “Lights in the air, above the visible layer?  Were they other planes, maybe from Vélane?”
   “No, ma’am.  Thes lights were very still, and very high, I’d estimate over a hundred miles above the surface. Nothing up there would hold them up.”
   “What is this large disk?”
   “The second light.  It grew brighter and more distinct as I ascended. I am calling it Neith, second ground.”
   “You know we can’t tell anyone.  They’ll think we’re crazy.”
   “Avel, these new freighters are going to dominate intercity transit soon, and every pilot will see what I reported.  And after that there will be passenger models.  What are you going to say when a thousand people see one every night?  People will say this is crazy but then they will all see it.”
***
   The hundred lights had become a sensation over the last several years.  A new branch of “science” had broken out.  Mostly it was just people making charts of where the lights were, but eventually true science came of it.  Some moved, some didn’t.  The light was different. They made spectra of the points.  
   Dr. Sélaire had a different view. “We need a permanent observatory. One place where we can steadily watch the lights.  Bigger cameras. More cameras.”
   Dr. Serand shook his head. “We’d all love that, but there is no place stable where we can see them.”
   “I know a place. Neith.”
***
   It had been forty years since Dr. Sélaire had suggested the Neith observatory.  No one knew how to get there. Flight was easy, but rocketry and building a habitat on Neith were not. Tirian worked in the Sélaire Observatory on Neith, along with about forty other astronomers. 
   When she stepped up on the podium, she brought up an image of the third moving light. “We believe this moving light is our twin.  It has measurable air, and an ocean-like liquid on the land.”
   “Does it have life?”
   “We don’t know for sure, but it seems very unlikely. The air is so thin you can see right through it, and the ocean is not made of CO2, it’s water.  We doubt anything could survive.”
***
   Three weeks later, Dalon was looking at the charts.  “Tirian, look at these two photos, taken a day apart. Do you see this light? In the second photo, I thought it was gone.  But it’s not.  It’s this light,” he said, pointing at a brighter light two squares over.”
   “What is that?  There are two kinds of lights: ones that orbit the prime light and ones that never move at all.  This is neither.”
   “I don’t know.  I think it is falling.”
   “Why would it fall?”
   “I don’t know, but I worked out the path. It is coming towards us.”
   “How big is it?”
   “I’m not sure, but it looks like about half the size of Neith.”
***
   “Falling Light Coming” the headlines blazed. President Tavon broadcast a message.  
   “Citizens of Avinel, this is Tirian, our foremost scientist from the Sélaire Observatory on Neath. Dr. Tirian, is it true that the falling light is going to hit us?”
   “We can’t say that for sure.  We know it will come close.”
   “If it hits, what happens?”
   “We think it will burn up in the air before it even gets to the visible layer.  We are doing analyses, but it is still under investigation.”
   Every day after that, a new chart was published showing the track and the expectations. The cone got tighter and tighter, and Venus was still in it. The problem was, there was absolutely nothing they could do about it.
   A week before impact the cone had tightened, and it looked like a miss.  While people cheered, it was still close. The Sélaire Observatory sent video constantly. People watched its closest approach, a thousand miles from the planet, and cheered. 
   Tirian and Dalon saw it differently. They held hands, until the falling light struck.  Neith, the observatory, and all the staff were completely destroyed in an instant.
***
   Neath became a field of debris.  Most of the large pieces fell to the surface within  a week.  The impact broke the crust, causing volcanoes.  It was a month before the scientists were able to understand.  There was no reason to tell the population, but as time passed, they realized there was no reason not to. 
   President Travon got online again with another live video. Citizens of Avinel, and Citizens of all fo Venus, I have spoken to our scientists.  As Neith continues to fall on us, the air is becoming thick with sulfuric acid.  Our civilization will die, and, in a couple hundred years, the acid in the air will have removed all trace of us.”
