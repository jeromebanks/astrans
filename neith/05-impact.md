# Neith

## Chapter Five: Impact

It grew from a point to a coin to something with no edge at all in less time than it took Talis to say Sar's name.

Nobody ran. There was nowhere on the whole small grey body that qualified as somewhere else. Osei's hand found Talis's glove and held it, and she held his back, and the last thing she had time to think, with the whole sky going the color of the inside of a furnace, was that Pell would get the four unfinished pages sitting on the desk in the dome and would have to guess at everything Talis had meant to say in them.

Then the light took everything.

***

Pell heard it first as good news.

She was in the flight academy's common hall when the broadcast cut across every screen at once, the College director's voice pitched too high, cracking on the second word: *Venus is safe.* The room came up out of its seats in a single motion, a noise Pell had never heard a room make before, half a cheer and half something closer to a sob, strangers grabbing each other by the shoulders, someone behind her saying *thank god, thank god* into the back of Pell's coat like a prayer.

She was cheering too, for the first four seconds, before the director's voice kept going and the second half of the sentence arrived.

*The object passed clear of Venus. It struck Neith directly. We have lost contact with the surface station. We believe there were no survivors.*

The room did not stop celebrating. That was the part Pell would never be able to explain to anyone who hadn't stood in it. Half the hall kept cheering, kept crying with relief, kept grabbing at each other, because their homes were safe and their children were safe and the sky above every city on the planet was still exactly where it had always been, and the other half, smaller, scattered through the crowd like Pell was, stood very still with the six names scrolling up the screen and could not make their bodies do either thing, could not celebrate and could not yet believe there was anything left to grieve, because grieving required first admitting the sentence was true.

Bren, T. First on the list. The names went in the order the College had them on file, and Talis's had always come first, ever since flight school, an accident of an alphabet that had never mattered to Pell until this exact moment.

Someone put a hand on her shoulder. She didn't turn to see who.

***

By evening the streets outside the academy were full in a way Pell had never seen them, not for any festival, not for any Sun-tide or Neith-tide the calendar had ever offered. People had come out simply to be near other people, to look at each other and confirm that the world was still the shape it had been that morning. Somewhere two streets over a crowd was singing something Pell didn't recognize, off-key and joyful, and closer, just outside the academy gates, a smaller group stood with six paper lanterns unlit in their hands, waiting for full dark to light them, because that was the old custom for the dead and nobody had thought to invent a new one yet.

Pell didn't join either group. She walked home instead, the long way, past the memorial board already going up outside the College's east entrance, six names and six faces printed too fast and slightly blurred, Talis's photo the one from her first solo flight, laughing at something outside the frame.

At her mother's door she stopped, hand on the latch, and looked up out of habit, the way she always had, the way everyone on the planet always had, their whole lives, whenever the weight of something got too large to carry alone.

The Ceiling looked exactly the way it always looked. Soft. Grey. Lit from nowhere anyone could name. Giving nothing back.

It had never once, in the whole history of anyone looking up at it, told a single person anything true about what was happening on the other side of it, and it did not start now. Somewhere beyond that unbroken grey, Neith was already coming apart, and there was no one left alive up there to send word of it, and there would be no way, for a long time yet, for anyone standing where Pell was standing to know.

She went inside to tell her mother.
