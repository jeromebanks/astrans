# Neith: Story Notes

Standalone story, unconnected to the Astrans universe. Several chapters, not a full novel.

## Premise

Venus is a beautiful planet with an advanced civilization. Its atmosphere is permanently thick
and overcast, so Venusians have only ever known two lights in the sky: the Sun and Neith, their
moon. High-altitude flyers occasionally report odd, flickering lights, actual stars, faintly
visible only at extreme altitude where the atmosphere thins. No one can explain them from the
surface.

Curiosity drives a scientific expedition to Neith itself, where the sky is clear. There, the
Venusians make first real contact with the scale of the universe: stars, distances, structure.
It is the high point of their civilization's understanding of itself and its place in the
cosmos.

Then they spot something that doesn't belong: a massive asteroid, inbound, unstoppable. Venus
has no way to deflect or evacuate from it. Both worlds wait.

It misses Venus. It hits Neith instead, killing everyone on the expedition and shattering the
moon into countless fragments. Venus mourns its lost scientists and, in the same breath,
celebrates its own survival.

The celebration is premature. Neith's debris field does not stay in orbit. Over years and
generations, fragments deorbit and strike Venus, one after another, triggering escalating
seismic shocks and volcanism. Sulfur floods the atmosphere. Piece by piece, as Neith disappears
into Venus, the planet becomes the sulfuric-acid hellscape it is today, and every trace of the
civilization that once lived there is erased.

## Framing device

Opens and closes on real historical fact, styled as a Wikipedia quote, about the actual
17th-18th century astronomical mystery of "Neith," a moon of Venus reported by multiple
astronomers between roughly 1645 and 1770, and never confirmed to exist:

- Opening: "Neith was observed up to 30 times by astronomers until 1770."
- Closing: "After 1770, there were no new sightings and it was not found during the transit of
  Venus in 1761 and 1769."

The gap between the clinical, external historical record and the intimate, catastrophic truth
it's actually describing is the emotional engine of the piece. Do not resolve or soften that
irony; it's the point.

## Terminology

- Demonym: Venusian (not Venetian).

## Tone

Blend: Astrans-style grounding for science and character scenes (close third person,
procedural texture, restraint over lyricism, emotion delivered through action), reserving more
lyrical, elegiac language for the framing quotes and the final chapter's slow apocalypse.

## Proposed chapter breakdown

Not yet finalized, pending confirmation.

1. **Wonder** — the perpetual overcast, the Sun-and-Neith cosmology, a flyer glimpsing
   impossible lights at altitude, the case made to send scientists to Neith.
2. **Ascent** — the expedition arrives and sets up on Neith. Introduce the protagonist(s), with
   something back on Venus worth losing.
3. **The Sky Opens** — the team maps the universe for the first time. Peak wonder. A strange
   point of light that doesn't behave like the others.
4. **The Thing That Won't Miss** — confirmed asteroid, no technology to stop it, the math, the
   wait on both worlds.
5. **Impact** — it misses Venus, hits Neith. The expedition dies. Venus grieves and celebrates
   in the same breath.
6. **The Long Death** — the debris field deorbits over years or generations, volcanism and acid
   clouds build, the civilization is erased, closing on the real historical line about 1770 and
   the missed transits.
