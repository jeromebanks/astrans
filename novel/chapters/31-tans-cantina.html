Chapter 31

Tan’s Cantina

1,923 words · about 8 minutes

Current Production14 tons / month

Li found Darrin working on the ovens. “Ready to go check it out?”

“Just a minute. I needed to replace the drive belt. I will be ready in five.”

“Don’t worry, Darrin, I’ve got it,” Bill said.

Darrin passed the wrench to Bill. “Thanks. I am being summoned.”

He then looked at Li. “Is it ready for a walk through?”

“It’s overdue.”

They hooked up to the cableway and glided across the crater floor, about twenty miles to the prominence. Darrin looked at the phase of the Earth. Ten days to dawn.

They got off the industrial cableway and hooked onto an ornate cable that went up a well-lit path.

“I didn’t expect it to be so bright,” Li said.

Darrin looked at the enormous dome. “They added titanium dioxide to the mix. It looks like marble.”

The entrance was a large revolving door. There was a large emergency airlock beside it.

“These revolving doors are going to be a maintenance nightmare,” Darrin said.

“Ellie told me they did this at Bounty. It’s elegant.”

Near the entrance, a simple line drawing of her face hung over a sign: “Welcome to Tan’s Cantina.”

They walked past the empty bar. Li checked sight lines, noted the mirrored wall behind the counter, tested the Earth window placement from different angles. The window was set into the dome’s northern curve, two meters across. She looked at the full Earth, imagining the shaft of blue-white light it would cast across the floor when the lights were dimmed.

On the stage, a smooth jazz trio from Chicago was practicing.

“How long are they here?” Darrin asked.

“Three weeks here, then one week at Fletcher’s Antipodes before they return here for a closing show.”

“Fletcher built a club?” Darrin asked.

“I hear it’s nice. Much smaller, of course. He should build more. Miners need someplace to visit.”

“They need a haven.”

Helen came in. “Tan, Amazon sent another message. They want a million square feet.”

“I’ll site them a couple miles from the depot. It will be fine. But I want you to word the response like it is a huge problem,” Li said. “We are still negotiating the rent.”

“What’s up with Amazon?” Darrin asked.

“With Fletcher connecting the Moon, Amazon thinks they can get most deliveries to customers in a day. Helen, what would you have done if you and Bill could have had a delivery in a day?”

“We would have replaced the airlock and been fine. We would still be mining.”

“The whole Moon needs that, but we don’t need to tell them that, yet.”

Helen left, and Li and Darrin kept walking around the cantina. They stepped out the east balcony.

“Do you like having Case out there? Without them it is an unspoiled view.”

“I like having them. Easier to buy new equipment, and now there are mechanics.”

“Komatsu and Sany Group also want to open dealerships.”

“There’s plenty of space. We could handle ten manufacturers. My only concern is that we can kick them out if their equipment is no good.”

“It’s still my crater.”

They continued on the balcony until they were looking south. “The row houses look very comfortable. Helen loves hers. Things are growing so fast. Where are you hiring from?”

“I rarely get anyone under the cableway anymore. I have a deal with Andrei. Whenever someone is calling it quits on the Moon, he lets me know and I go talk to them. Some are happy to stay if they can be someplace like this: steady work, nice housing, power, hospitality, and community. For me, I love our hut. I think we chose well when we bought it, and it has grown on me since.”

Li glanced at him quickly. “I want to show you the hotel. Have you looked at it?”

“Only from the outside. Let’s see it.”

Li led him inside, and they walked through a few rooms. They had no windows, just small monitors in each room with a current view of Earth. She brought him to the top floor.

“Look at this room, Darrin. What do you think?”

Darrin went around the apartment. High ceilings. A real Earth window. The bedroom had a door and a full sized bed. It had a separate shower room with a bathtub as well. Kitchen, office, and a large room that was really just for nothing. You could sit there and talk.

“What is this room for? Visiting dignitaries? It is so extravagant. No one can afford this, Li.”

“We can. I built this for us. The area where our hut is interferes with the shopping mall I have planned. We could dig it out and move it, but I think this may be nicer.”

Darrin showed only his practical face. The tiny hut on the north edge of the prominence had been their home since they first arrived at Tycho crater. “If you like, Li.”

Li looked away. “Darrin, I need to stop by home before we go to the opening. I can’t be Tan tonight wearing a pressure suit.”

They took a rover to their home. Li asked Darrin to wait outside. Darrin felt alone when she entered the airlock. She was quick. Keeping her pressure suit and helmet on, she grabbed a large garment bag and came back out.

“OK, I’m ready,” Li said, getting back on the rover. Darrin drove them to a cableway station, and they took the cableway up to the Cantina.

Back in her office, Li opened the bag and took out a black silk qipao with red and gold embroidery and a simple suit. “Let’s get changed,” she said as she began undressing.

“Me, too?” Darrin asked, as he began changing. “I should comb my hair.”

When they were ready, they stepped out into the cantina. Lights were low. The full Earth shone brightly through the Earth window, casting a dazzling beam of bluish light on the floor. There was a table stacked with champagne from France and another with bubbly amaranth chichas from Mars. Li picked up the oversized scissors from behind the bar and they went to the elevator landing where the miners were waiting behind the red ribbon.

“Welcome to the all-new Tan’s Cantina!” she said cutting the ribbon, letting the miners in. They flooded through, whooping and cheering as the band began playing “Moonland”, a reworked version of “Birdland.”

Darrin was immediately caught up by various friends he had met as he worked on the cableway. He sipped a chicha while talking to Rook. “Didn’t expect to see you here, Rook. I figured you’d avoid the crowds and drop by a week later,” Darrin said with a wink.

“What, and miss my one chance of seeing you all gussied up in a suit? Ha!” Rook replied. “Nice tie.” Rook smirked and took two chichas and went to find some roast cavy.

Darrin blushed a little, but it was a nice suit and the dark red tie looked great. He talked to some guys from Ocean State Resources. Most of the miners were in their singlets they wore under their pressure suits, but Ocean State miners put on jumpsuits over that. A little more formal. They had almost completed their second claim, and were talking about starting a new claim beyond the cableway.

Li was in a different world. Several corporate representatives had come up from Earth for this event. Amazon had sent a representative, Case had, but it was Mark Davis from David Clark Company that caught her attention first, eager to get up here before Oceaneering. “Tan, have you seen our new pressure suits? They are much more durable than what most people are using up here. I was thinking, what if we set up a storefront, nothing huge, out next to Case? Someplace miners could find replacements or upgrades.”

“How much space would you need?” she asked, playing a little more naive than she really was.

Mark pulled out his slate. “A showroom for a few different models and components, a couple dressing rooms, a workspace where we can refit and repair suits, and storage space. Total is about a thousand feet. If you had unpressurized warehouse space, we would probably like that much again.”

Li laughed when he mentioned dressing rooms. Earthers were so self conscious. “Yeah, I think we can accommodate that. Unpressurized space is easy. I will set that up in the north, near Amazon. Your retail space will be ready in two months, connected to the cantina. Two hundred dollars per foot, plus twenty percent of the gross. I will give you a premium spot near the entrance.”

Mark didn’t know what to expect on the space, but her taking a cut of the gross was not something his company would like. “How much would it be if we just paid upfront, no cut of the gross?”

“Oh, that would be fine. Tycho Crater is not for everyone. Do you have a claim?”

“No, I mean here. Here is where we want to be.”

“OK. Because here in my claim I can get you a thousand feet of premium mall space for two hundred dollars per foot plus twenty percent of the gross. Interested?”

“Say we were. Tell me about the space we would get.”

Tan led him to her office. She brought up the mall plans on a projector. “Here is the mall we are planning. Twenty thousand square feet on two floors. The main door will be facing north. First building from the depot and there is a cableway stop there. When they enter, you will be the first thing they see. I will let you stay there as long as you are the top performing business in the mall.”

“As long as we are the top performer?”

“Have you ever shopped in a grocery store? You notice that sometimes the canned food is on aisle one, but then you come back in a few months and it is on aisle two? I am not building a big mall here, and in that limited space I want companies that really make life better for miners. I will rearrange where the stores are to do that. Soon Oceaneering will be here, and Teijin Frontier will want a space. And customers will decide which of you is most important.”

“Customers? A minute ago you said sales.”

“Yes, well, sales are how customers tell me which company is most important.”

“OK, Tan, you win. We have to be here. Just give me the papers, and I will sign the agreement.”

“Papers?” Tan looked through her Earth window. “Earth is adorable. On Earth, papers would make a lease enforceable in court. But this is the Moon. No court. No papers. We agree, and you can move in as soon as the structure is built.”

Mark and Li returned to the cantina. Mark took a glass of champagne, thinking about how this would go over when he called the home office. They were not going to like the terms. They would blame him, as if he ever had a choice. He downed the first glass and picked up another.

Darrin caught her eye. He motioned to the floor. “May I have this dance?”

Li blushed and demurred. “Let’s just stand here next to each other.”

As she watched the other miners in the shaft of earthlight, it struck her. Tycho Crater had become a city. The only real city off Earth.
